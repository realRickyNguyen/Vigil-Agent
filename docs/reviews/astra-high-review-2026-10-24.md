# Vigil design review (Astra High, 24 Oct 2026)

## Summary

Vigil is a useful prototype for assembling a first-pass review packet, but I would not yet trust its queue to allocate a drug-safety team's attention without reviewing the exclusions as well (**inference** from the selection and priority gates in `app/agents/analyst.py:19-25,38-51` and `app/agents/reviewer.py:51-68`). Its strongest features are inspectable statistics, extracted label evidence, and explicit unfinished states, all present in the two completed dossiers (**verified**, M and L below: `review_queue`, `evidence_queries`, `counts`). Its central weakness is treating an unlabelled event as the main reason to investigate and a labelled event or treated condition as a reason to lower priority, although a signal can concern a new aspect of an established risk (**verified code**, `reviewer.py:64-68`; **external standard**, [EMA GVP Module IX, IX.A.1](https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/documents/scientific-guideline/guideline-good-pharmacovigilance-practices-gvp-module-ix-signal-management-rev-1_en.pdf)). The outputs demonstrate useful questions, but also accepted conclusions whose own objections undermine them, particularly metformin blood glucose decreased and metabolic acidosis (**verified**, M findings' `reviewer`, `strongest_objection`, and `history`). The supplied evaluation does not demonstrate added classification accuracy from the Reviewer: it retained five wrong first-round answers and recorded no fixes (**verified**, `eval/reports/eval-20261023T125018Z-gold_v2.md:14-20`). The Skeptic adds real evidence, but its threshold crossing is a sensitivity check, not an explanation of causation or a reliable reason to demote a signal (**verified mechanism**, `app/agents/skeptic.py:77-118`; **inference** about interpretation). I would retain the evidence assembly and bounded review workflow, then spend the next effort on clinical prioritisation, label interpretation and a human-adjudicated evaluation before adding more agent debate (**recommendation** based on the findings below).

## 1. Fit in the signal-management workflow

**Inferred workflow fit:** use Vigil as a statistical-alert review assistant between detection and human validation. It can prepare candidate associations and organise questions. A finding marked `reviewed` means its model Reviewer accepted it (`pipeline.py:179-188`); it does not establish that a safety professional validated a signal.

| Stage | What Vigil contributes | What remains outside the evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Detection | **Verified:** report-based PRR and Yates-corrected chi-squared, followed by an Evans screen (`faers.py:85-105`; `pipeline.py:53-55`). | **Inferred limitation:** this detects selected statistical associations. The top-999 joins, count threshold and candidate limits leave blind spots; there is no alternative route for a compelling rare event below the screen. |
| Validation | **Verified:** a selected label, code checks and objections (`reviewer.py:40-69`; M/L findings). | **Inferred limitation:** no case verification, chronology, dechallenge/rechallenge assessment or synthesis of independent clinical evidence is performed. A contraindication or indication hypothesis cannot determine what actually happened in a report. |
| Prioritisation | **Verified:** priority category, workflow state and chi-squared order (`pipeline.py:291-319`). | **Inferred limitation:** seriousness, preventability, affected population, changing reporting and risk-management implications are not ranking inputs. Chi-squared chiefly ranks strength of departure from the chosen reporting comparator, not clinical urgency. |
| Assessment | **Verified:** the dossier offers `next_check`, but the pipeline has no general mechanism to execute these checks (`pipeline.py:120-175`; M/L `next_check`). | **Inferred limitation:** it is a handoff to assessment, not an assessment conclusion. A sponsor case series, literature review or epidemiological study would supply evidence this pipeline does not contain. |

**External standard:** EMA's signal-management framework includes both newly suspected associations and changes to known associations, with prioritisation directed at potential patient or public-health impact. That is the benchmark for my workflow judgement, not a claim that this hackathon tool must implement an entire regulatory process. [EMA GVP Module IX](https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/documents/scientific-guideline/guideline-good-pharmacovigilance-practices-gvp-module-ix-signal-management-rev-1_en.pdf).

**Verified output, inferred usefulness:** M presents acute kidney injury, hyperkalaemia and weight decreased as its three accepted `review_next` findings. The co-drug counts and alternative explanations would help an analyst formulate an initial review. But those three findings are all ambiguous in the gold set, and their clinical priority has not been adjudicated (`gold_v2.jsonl:43,50-51`; evaluation report line 9). The tool has demonstrated that it can produce a review packet, not that this is the best use of the analyst's next hour.

**Verified output:** L's five accepted review-next items are sputum discoloured, full blood count abnormal, loss of personal independence, pneumonia and bronchitis. Its counts show 20 assessed out of 257 screen-positive reactions; M shows 15 out of 140. Those are approximately 7.8% and 10.7%, respectively, calculated from the dossier `counts`. **Inference:** a polished shortlist can obscure how little of the positive screen received substantive review, even though the coverage counts are honestly displayed.

**Verified overreach in generated advice:** M's metabolic-acidosis `next_check` proposes comparing a FAERS reporting rate with labelled incidence; L's urticaria objection compares PRR with clinical-trial frequencies. Those are incompatible denominators. FDA states that spontaneous reports cannot establish incidence. **Recommendation:** reject this type of comparison and label any external incidence comparison as requiring another dataset. [FDA dashboard limitations](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/fdas-adverse-event-reporting-system-faers/fda-adverse-event-reporting-system-faers-public-dashboard).

## 3. Agent roles and critique loop

**Verified architecture:** Data Clerk and Skeptic are deterministic components; Analyst and Reviewer make model calls. Both recorded runs use the same Nemotron Ultra model for the two model roles (TM/TL `run_start`). Both roles receive the same lexical label retrieval (`analyst.py:172-175`; `reviewer.py:129-137`). **Inference:** these are different instructions over largely shared evidence, not independent corroboration.

**Verified strengths:** the Reviewer can catch copied-number errors and false literal citations; the loop can request additional reactions and leave disputes unresolved (`reviewer.py:40-69`; `pipeline.py:81-119,179-192`). The Skeptic obtains additional co-drug evidence, which a single static prompt could not generate without tool access (`skeptic.py:166-192`). Those are meaningful functions. Their value does not require describing every deterministic step as an agent.

**Verified limited benefit in this evaluation:** the report records `kept_correct 57`, `kept_wrong 5`, `not_reviewed 3`, with no `fixed` cases (report line 16). Its 59/65 versus 60/65 overall accuracy is not a paired one-case correction: the accepted final set differs from the first-round set (`score.py:55-65,83-100`). M/L had no rejected first-round findings; L named three additional reactions, all of which failed classification (`rounds`, `counts`, and failed queue entries). **Inference:** the present evidence supports some coverage and documentation value, but not a demonstrated improvement in clinical judgement.

**Verified agreement caveat:** all 20 recorded model calls in M and 26 of 27 in L had memory injected (`model_calls`). **Inference:** repeated-run agreement cannot be interpreted as an independent reproducibility experiment. The content and effect of the injected memories were not determined here; these counts do not prove contamination or copying.

**Observed critique without consequence:** M's metabolic-acidosis objection says the cited contraindication is not an adverse-effect description, yet the Reviewer accepts it. Its low-glucose objection supplies a competing hypoglycaemia interpretation but accepts intended therapeutic improvement. L's full-blood-count objection calls the term nonspecific, yet accepts `review_next` despite the prompt's instruction to reject vague escalations (`reviewer.py:25`; corresponding finding histories). **Inference:** generating an objection has become a prose requirement rather than a requirement to resolve a material uncertainty.

**Recommendations to make review substantive:**

1. Give the Reviewer an independent evidence task before showing the Analyst's verdict: find the strongest supporting and contradicting label passages, identify whether each describes an effect, risk factor, indication or contraindication, and record unresolved scope mismatches. It should request another passage when retrieval is inadequate. This directly addresses the shared failure in TM seq 35/79.
2. Require a disposition for every material objection: resolved by cited evidence, changes the decision, or remains open and requires human review. Require an explicit valid verdict for every requested reaction; missing output must remain unreviewed. This addresses `reviewer.py:124-126` and the accepted contradictions above.
3. Turn the Skeptic into a choice of evidence checks driven by the hypothesis. For a disease-like respiratory term, use indication and comparator sensitivities; for a suspected reporting burst, use time and reporter source; for renal/metabolic findings, examine relevant co-reported conditions and drugs. Report what each check can and cannot resolve. All new numerical evidence should continue through Zetaris.
4. Return material co-drug findings to the final reasoning even when adjusted PRR stays above 2. Show stratum counts, uncertainty, attenuation and possible effect modification; use “no threshold-crossing challenge” instead of `holds`. Do not infer an interaction mechanism from Omega or causal exoneration from attenuation.
5. Compare rules plus one classification pass against rules plus Reviewer and rules plus Reviewer/Skeptic on held-out, adjudicated tasks. Measure errors corrected, errors introduced, relevant items missed, evidence quality and analyst time. The current report does not establish which configuration is worth its latency.

**Inferred answer to the single-prompt question:** plain code can handle the arithmetic, workflow bookkeeping and literal checks; a constrained model is useful for semantic matching and proposing alternatives. A single classification prompt with these code checks is a credible baseline. The present files do not establish that it would match or outperform the full loop, so I would test that proposition rather than claim either architecture is necessary.

## 4. Decision contract

**Verified positive design choice:** separating coverage, context, priority and workflow status is better than the older single classification (`schemas.py:3-18`). **Inferred weakness:** the implementation couples them again through the priority gate, while `insufficient_evidence` mixes evidence adequacy with urgency. A serious but poorly documented event can need urgent information gathering.

| Question | Recommended change | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| What does the reference label cover? | Keep coverage, but add the exact reference scope and a relation such as exact, synonym, broader, narrower, conditional, mentioned without attribution, or absent after adequate search. Distinguish unavailable/not retrieved from searched and absent. | **Verified gaps:** `schemas.py:48-60`; `label.py:91-134`; source-selection mismatch in M/L. **Recommendation:** avoid forcing partial or conditional coverage into a binary result. |
| What is this term, and what might explain it? | Separate event type from explanatory hypotheses. Permit both a clinical event and possible indication/disease explanation. Add unknown, therapeutic effect, medication-use issue and consequence-of-event where needed. | **Verified:** current mutually exclusive enum and broad treated-condition definition, `schemas.py:13`; `analyst.py:38-46`. **Inference:** those categories cannot represent mixed report populations. |
| Why should someone review it, and how soon? | Separate urgency from evidence sufficiency. Record the trigger: suspected new association, new aspect of a known risk, treatment failure, pregnancy exposure, interaction, or unresolved data/label question. Permit human escalation below Evans thresholds. | **Verified:** current gate excludes these combinations, `reviewer.py:51-68`. **Recommendation:** rank using clinical consequence and decision need, not novelty alone. |
| What has actually been completed? | Keep workflow status, but identify code-checked, model-reviewed and human-validated separately; require a valid response and record outstanding evidence tasks. | **Verified:** `settle()` maps model accept to reviewed, `pipeline.py:183-188`, with the Reviewer fallthrough noted above. |

**Recommended minimum evidence packet:** the existing finding contains `a`, PRR and a label citation but lacks the following structured evidence (`schemas.py:48-60`; `pipeline.py:303-307`). These are questions an analyst would need answered or explicitly marked unavailable before using the rank:

- **Population and statistics:** exact ingredient/product scope, role of the drug in reports, period, data version, all four contingency cells, comparator definition and an uncertainty measure. `Signal` already holds the four cells, but the main finding does not retain them (`schemas.py:36-44`). Reporting ratios must remain distinct from incidence.
- **Clinical importance:** seriousness/outcomes, age distribution and vulnerable populations, plus whether the suspected novelty concerns severity, persistence, timing or preventability. Aggregate seriousness is a report characteristic; it must not be attributed to a particular co-reported reaction without qualification.
- **Case support:** identifiable case series for expert follow-up, chronology, dose, dechallenge/rechallenge, relevant co-reported events, missingness and duplicate assessment. These are proposed evidence requests, not data currently present in the app.
- **Label scope and entailment:** complete supporting passage, section, product/formulation/age scope and qualifiers. A matching substring should not be the evidence standard for a clinical relationship.
- **Actionability:** one executable next step, its data source/access requirement, owner and result that would change the decision. L's full-blood-count next check requests narratives that this app does not have; M's requested renal-function stratification is not a measured renal-function dataset.

**External limitation:** FDA explains that public dashboard data omit narrative details its clinical reviewers use for case definitions, duplicate removal and causality assessment. **Recommendation:** distinguish checks feasible with public structured reports from those requiring sponsor records, requested reports or another source; do not imply public openFDA aggregates can answer all the generated questions. [FDA explanation of postmarket surveillance data](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/cder-conversations/understanding-cders-postmarket-safety-surveillance-programs-and-public-data).

## 5. Gold labels

**Verified strengths:** the labelling rules identify who labelled the data, record reference-label versions, preserve v1, and flag ambiguity (`LABELLING_RULES.md:5-9,27-41,48-60`). **Inferred limitation:** this is an AI-authored development reference for document interpretation. It is not a reference standard for clinically worthwhile review-next decisions.

**Verified evaluation limitations:** reactions were selected from the top 20 by chi-squared for five drugs (`LABELLING_RULES.md:11-15`); 83 v2 decisions were retained from v1, and some earlier outputs had been seen (`57-60`). The report's blanket description of other categories as unsupported escalations (`report:22`; `score.py:167-168`) is clinically too strong.

**Verified methodological inconsistency:** the rules allow broad label concepts to cover events but offer no calibrated boundary for breadth (`LABELLING_RULES.md:22`). L's anxiety finding uses the very broad phrase “neuropsychiatric events” as coverage, whereas full blood count abnormal remains unclear when narrower haematological findings are listed. **Recommendation:** adjudicate the actual relation and qualifiers rather than assume that membership of a broad medical domain makes an event adequately described. For sertraline sexual dysfunction (gold row 95), the cited specific sexual effects alone are insufficient to audit broad coverage; check the complete label before changing that row.

**Recommended evaluation revision:** have a pharmacovigilance reviewer and a second adjudicator label coverage relation, competing context hypotheses, action/urgency and evidence adequacy separately. Keep uncertain cases in a reported stratum with acceptable alternatives rather than making them disappear from the main account of usefulness. Add held-out drugs and cases selected for clinical importance, including known-risk changes, exposure problems and rare events outside the current screen. Report missed actionable items, inappropriate closure and queue usefulness separately from semantic classification accuracy. These are recommendations; no human-comparison study is present in the reviewed material.

## Open questions

- **Deployment decision:** is the intended user screening a particular sponsor product, an ingredient across formulations, or a hospital medication-safety population? README `28-39` gives a broad user story, but these uses require different references and escalation routes. I could not determine the intended operational boundary.
- **Clinical adjudication:** who will approve the gold decisions and review what the system suppresses? The available rules explicitly say no person has reviewed the reference set (`LABELLING_RULES.md:7,60`). No analyst time-saving or decision-quality study was provided.
- **Historical label completeness:** where are the complete frozen labels used to build v2? The traces preserve shown excerpts, and the gold preserves quotations/provenance, but I did not independently verify every absent term across each full source label. `eval/build_gold_v2.py:12` refers to exported worksheets; the reviewed `eval/` file listing did not contain them.
- **Data snapshots:** do baseline, per-drug and pair-count sources refresh together, and are query result bodies retained elsewhere? The inspected trace query events record SQL and a row count, not complete label/result payloads. The app does not enforce a common snapshot (`faers.py:209-220,274-291`); actual Zetaris caching behaviour was not tested.
- **Hidden model context:** what text did Nightshift inject, and can evaluation runs be isolated? M/L establish injection counts, not the content's influence. Same-model agreement and repeat-run agreement should remain qualified until this is understood.
- **Clinical information access:** can the user obtain structured case records or narratives for the suggested follow-up? Several output checks depend on information outside the current aggregates. Availability of that evidence determines how actionable the packet is.
- **Current-version performance:** do the 24 October changes fix the previous examples without suppressing valuable functional or exposure signals? The requested 23 October outputs cannot answer that, and I intentionally did not inspect the ongoing run or execute another one.
- **Calibration:** what proportion of suggested reviews would a safety professional actually pursue, and which important associations were never selected? Neither conditional classification accuracy nor the two supplied queues establishes those quantities.
